Christian marriage coaching vs. counseling: what is the difference?

Two kinds of help, two different jobs

Waymark Inc. · November 27, 2026

“Two are better than one… for if they fall, one will lift up the other.”

Ecclesiastes 4:9–10

Couples who are struggling often search for “marriage counseling” because it is the only name they know for getting help. Coaching is a different kind of help, and knowing the difference saves you time, money and disappointment.

What a licensed counselor does

A marriage counselor or therapist holds a state license. That license allows them to diagnose and treat mental health conditions such as depression, anxiety, trauma and addiction. Much of their work looks at where a problem came from and how to treat it.

What a marriage coach does

A coach does not diagnose or treat anything. Coaching is practical, structured work on the relationship itself: how the two of you talk, how you handle conflict, how trust is rebuilt, and what each of you will do before the next session. It looks mostly at where you are today and where you want to be.

Christian marriage coaching adds one more thing. It stands on Scripture and on the conviction that God’s plan for a broken marriage is restoration.

How to tell which one you need

See a licensed professional first if either of you is dealing with a mental health condition, an addiction, thoughts of self-harm, or the effects of trauma.

Get to safety first if there is violence or you are afraid of your spouse. In the United States you can call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233.

Coaching fits when the two of you are distant, stuck in the same argument, recovering from broken trust, or simply want a stronger marriage and a plan to build it.

Sometimes it is both. A couple can see a counselor for one need and work with a coach on the marriage at the same time.
