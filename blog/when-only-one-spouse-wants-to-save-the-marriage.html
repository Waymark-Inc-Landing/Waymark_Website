When only one of you wants to save the marriage

What one person can do, and what one person cannot

Waymark Inc. · November 27, 2026

“Let us not grow weary of doing good, for in due season we will reap, if we do not give up.”

Galatians 6:9

It is one of the loneliest places in a marriage: you still want it, and your husband or wife says they are done. You did not choose this alone, and you cannot fix it alone. But what you do next still matters a great deal.

What one person cannot do

You cannot make another person stay, feel, or change. Pleading, pressure and promises made in a panic usually push a tired spouse further away. Accepting this limit is not giving up. It is where honest work begins.

What one person can do

Stop the pursuit. Fewer long messages, fewer arguments about the relationship. Give room without going cold.

Own your part, and only your part. Name it plainly, without asking for anything back.

Change something they can see. Not a speech about changing. One real change, kept for weeks.

Be steady. Kind, calm and consistent at home, with the children and about money, even when nothing comes back.

Do not carry it alone. Pray, and let a pastor, a mature friend or a coach walk with you. Avoid people who only tell you what you want to hear.

Leave the door open. Say once, clearly, that you want the marriage and are willing to do the work. Then let your life say it.

How long?

Nobody can promise you an outcome. Some marriages are restored after one person held steady for months. Others are not. What we can say is that a person who does this work comes out clearer, steadier and closer to God, whatever their spouse decides.

An important limit

None of this means staying where you are in danger. If there is violence or you are afraid, get to safety first. In the United States, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233.

You can start alone

Many of the people we coach begin by themselves, because their spouse is not ready. That is a good place to start. See marriage restoration coaching, or read how trust is rebuilt. If the marriage has already ended, there is a path for that too: rebuilding after divorce.
